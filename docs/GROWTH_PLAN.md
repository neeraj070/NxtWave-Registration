# CampusAI workshop growth plan

**Status: planning assumptions, not validated results.** The campaign is a seven-day simulation with a ₹2,000 ceiling and a goal of 500 final-year engineering student registrations. The app does not contact students or claim those registrations occurred.

## Audience and value

Target final-year engineering students across India, including students who have AI curiosity but limited experience turning concepts into a project. Likely barriers include uncertainty about where to start, a belief that AI projects need advanced experience, and low confidence choosing a practical first idea. The value proposition is a free, focused, beginner-friendly online workshop: move from curiosity to a first practical AI project in 60 minutes. Do not promise a date, joining link, certificate, or career outcome before confirmation.

## Channel priorities and seven-day sequence

1. **Student ambassadors (first priority):** Give campus representatives trackable links and clear copy. Peer recommendations can reach students in a relevant campus network with more context and trust than a cold placement.
2. **College clubs (second priority):** Ask engineering and technical clubs to share the invitation. A club can distribute it to a concentrated student audience through an established campus channel.
3. **Student communities (third priority):** Share concise workshop information in relevant WhatsApp and other student groups, following group rules. These groups can spread a practical event quickly among peers who already share study interests.
4. **Follow-up and referral recovery:** Send at most one respectful reminder to interested students through the channel where they opted in. This can recover intent that was interrupted without treating every group member as a lead.
5. **Organic social:** Post a short, practical AI project demo and clear registration link. A concrete example makes the workshop outcome easier to understand and can be shared without media spend.
6. **Paid promotion (small experiment):** Run a capped test only after source tracking is checked. Paid reach can test audience and message fit, but its cost and uncertain conversion keep it below peer distribution.

Email is not in the initial target model because no opted-in student list is established. If one exists, use it only with appropriate consent and a tagged link; do not assume or purchase a list.

Reach and conversion are illustrative planning inputs only. They are not validated estimates. The recovery bucket is incremental only if it does not double-count registrations already attributed to their original source.

This is a capacity model, not a forecast: it assumes the listed reach and conversion inputs can be achieved and that recovery registrations are incremental. Validate the inputs with early channel results, then reallocate effort toward channels with sufficient observed registrations. The first three peer channels receive priority; paid spend remains capped.

The admin dashboard records spend separately from registration target allocation. Set spend to actual campaign outlay; do not infer it from the allocation. Pause and inspect a paid placement if it spends materially without verified registrations. Continue tracking all channels even if their initial target is zero. Never scale from a tiny sample.

## Measurement, risks, and optimization

Track unique registrations, qualified final-year share, valid referral attribution, channel source, daily pace, spend, and cost per registration when registrations are nonzero. The source precedence is a valid ambassador code first, then recognized UTM, then Direct or unknown; each SQLite row counts once. Compare actuals with planned counts as variance only, not as validation. Risks include unconfirmed event logistics, spam-like distribution, referral-code typos, UTM loss during link forwarding, duplicate submissions, channel overlap, and reach estimates that do not translate into registrations. Check consent and group rules before any real-world outreach.

## If I had another 24 hours

I would first check the registration and referral flow at mobile widths and keyboard-only, then run a small, consented channel pilot with unique links and record delivered reach and link visits. I would compare the observed registrations against those denominators, test one alternate workshop message, and revise the channel targets only after enough observations. These are proposed next steps; no pilot or message test is claimed to have happened.
