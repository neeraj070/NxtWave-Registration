# CampusAI Workshop Growth Plan

**Status: Planning assumptions, not validated results.**

The objective is to plan a seven-day campaign to generate **500 registrations from final-year engineering students** for the free workshop, *Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes*, with a maximum budget of **₹2,000**.

This is a simulated growth campaign. The prototype demonstrates the registration and attribution workflow; it does not contact students, execute the campaign, or claim that 500 registrations have been achieved.

## 1. Audience and Value Proposition

The target audience is final-year engineering students across India, particularly students who are curious about AI but have limited experience building practical projects.

Potential barriers include uncertainty about where to start, the belief that AI projects require advanced skills, and difficulty choosing a manageable first project.

The value proposition is a free, focused, beginner-friendly online workshop that helps students move from AI curiosity toward building their first practical AI project in 60 minutes.

Workshop dates, joining links, certificates, and career outcomes must not be promised until they are officially confirmed.

## 2. Channel Priorities

### Priority 1: Student Ambassadors

Recruit student representatives who can share the workshop with relevant campus networks. Provide each ambassador with clear promotional copy and a unique referral code or tagged link.

**Why:** Peer recommendations can make the invitation more relevant and credible than an unfamiliar advertisement.

### Priority 2: College Clubs

Approach engineering, coding, and technical clubs to request distribution through their established student channels.

**Why:** Clubs can reach concentrated audiences with shared academic and technical interests.

### Priority 3: Student Communities

Share concise workshop information in relevant WhatsApp and other student communities, following each group's rules.

**Why:** Existing student communities can help distribute a practical learning opportunity quickly.

### Priority 4: Organic Social Content

Publish a short demonstration of a simple AI project, accompanied by the workshop's value proposition and registration link.

**Why:** A concrete example helps students understand what they could learn and gives them something useful to share.

### Priority 5: Follow-up and Referral Recovery

Plan at most one respectful reminder for interested students through the original channel where they expressed interest or opted in.

**Why:** A reminder may help students who intended to register but did not finish.

The current prototype does not track referral-link clicks, so it cannot identify people who clicked but did not register. Follow-up registrations are therefore a planning target, not a measured recovery result. They must be counted only when verified and must not duplicate registrations already attributed to another channel.

### Priority 6: Paid Promotion

Run a small, capped promotion experiment only after checking source attribution and registration tracking.

**Why:** Paid promotion can test audience and message fit, but the cost and uncertain conversion make peer distribution the initial priority.

Email is excluded from the initial target model because an opted-in student list has not been established. If a suitable list becomes available, use it only with appropriate consent and tagged links. Do not purchase or assume access to a student mailing list.

## 3. Seven-Day Execution Plan

| Day | Planned activities |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Check the registration funnel, workshop copy, source attribution, and dashboard. |
| Day 2 | Recruit student ambassadors and approach relevant college clubs. |
| Day 3 | Begin planned community distribution using channel-specific tagged links. |
| Day 4 | Share a practical AI project demonstration to make the workshop outcome concrete. |
| Day 5 | Review verified registrations and prioritize channels with promising results. |
| Day 6 | Send a limited reminder to interested students through the original channel, where appropriate. |

This is a proposed execution schedule. No real-world campaign activity is claimed to have occurred.

## 4. Registration Target Model

The goal is to plan for 500 registrations across five sources.

| Channel | Planned registrations | Illustrative reach or audience | Illustrative conversion assumption |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Student ambassadors | 240 | 1,200 | 20% |
| College clubs | 120 | 800 | 15% |
| Student communities | 90 | 1,500 | 6% |
| Paid promotion | 20 | 2,000 | 1% |
| Follow-up and referral recovery | 30 | 150 interested students | 20% |
| **Total** | **500** | — | — |

All reach figures and conversion rates are illustrative planning inputs, not validated estimates or achieved results.

The model assumes that the proposed audiences can be reached and that the assumed conversion rates are achievable. The 30-registration recovery target is incremental only if those registrations are not already counted under their original source.

This is a **capacity model, not a validated forecast**. Actual results could be lower or higher. Early channel evidence must be used to revise the assumptions rather than treating the targets as guaranteed outcomes.

## 5. Budget Allocation and Spending Controls

The total planned budget is capped at ₹2,000.

| Budget category | Planned ceiling |
|---|---:|
| Paid promotion experiment | ₹800 |
| Distribution and creative | ₹700 |
| Performance reserve | ₹500 |
| **Total** | **₹2,000** |

### Budget controls

- **Paid promotion — ₹800:** Cap the experiment at this amount. The planning target is 20 paid registrations, equivalent to ₹40 per registration if the entire allocation is spent and all 20 registrations are achieved.
- **Distribution and creative — ₹700:** Plan for campus outreach materials, promotional copy, and creative assets that support ambassador, club, and community distribution.
- **Performance reserve — ₹500:** Keep this amount uncommitted initially. Reallocate it only after reviewing verified registrations and channel performance.
- Record actual campaign expenditure separately from budget allocations. An allocation is not evidence that money has been spent.
- Pause and review a paid placement if it incurs material spend without verified registrations.
- Do not increase spending or scale a channel based on a very small sample.
- Continue recording relevant channel sources, including sources with no initial registration target.

The ₹40 paid-registration figure is a target derived from the planned budget and registration goal. It is not an observed acquisition cost or a guarantee.

## 6. Measurement and Attribution

The proposed measurement framework includes:

- **Unique registrations:** Count each valid registration once.
- **Audience qualification:** Check the share of registrants who meet the final-year engineering student target.
- **Source attribution:** Record the identifiable source of each registration.
- **Referral attribution:** Attribute registrations to a valid ambassador code when one is provided.
- **Daily progress:** Compare verified registrations with the planned target and seven-day schedule.
- **Cost per registration:** Calculate actual spend divided by verified registrations when the denominator is greater than zero.
- **Channel performance:** Compare actual results with planned channel targets and revise assumptions when sufficient evidence is available.

### Source attribution rules

Use the following precedence for a registration:

1. A valid ambassador referral code.
2. A recognized UTM source when no valid ambassador code is present.
3. Direct or unknown when neither source can be established.

Each SQLite registration record counts once. Planned channel allocations are targets, not additional registrations.

The prototype does not currently implement referral-click tracking. Therefore, it cannot calculate referral-link click-to-registration conversion or identify individual non-registering link visitors. This is a future measurement improvement, not a current capability.

## 7. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Workshop logistics are not confirmed | Do not promise dates, joining links, certificates, or outcomes until confirmed. |
| Distribution becomes spam-like | Follow group rules and use limited, relevant outreach. |
| Referral codes contain errors | Validate codes and check attribution before reporting results. |
| UTM information is lost when links are forwarded | Test tagged links and interpret unattributed registrations conservatively. |
| Duplicate submissions inflate totals | Count unique, valid registration records according to the prototype's validation rules. |
| Channels overlap | Apply one source attribution per registration and avoid double-counting recovery registrations. |
| Reach assumptions do not translate into registrations | Review observed results and revise targets instead of treating assumptions as facts. |
| Paid promotion consumes budget without results | Keep paid spend capped and review placements against verified registrations. |
| Student information is used without appropriate consent | Collect only necessary information and follow applicable consent and privacy requirements before any real campaign. |

## 8. What I Would Do With Another 24 Hours

I would first test the registration flow at mobile screen widths and using keyboard-only navigation. I would then run a small, consented channel pilot with unique tagged links and record delivered reach, link visits where measurable, and completed registrations.

Next, I would test an alternative workshop message against the original message, keeping the audience and distribution conditions as comparable as possible. I would compare registration conversion rates, review attribution quality, and revise the channel targets only when enough observations were available.

I would also prioritize implementing referral-click tracking before attempting click-based recovery, so future follow-up decisions can be based on measurable behavior rather than assumptions.

These are proposed next steps. No pilot, message experiment, or campaign result is claimed to have occurred.

## 9. Final Decision

The initial strategy prioritizes student ambassadors, college clubs, and relevant student communities, with organic social content supporting distribution. Paid promotion is a capped experiment rather than the primary acquisition channel.

The 500-registration goal and ₹2,000 budget define the simulation's constraints. Success in a real campaign would need to be assessed using verified registrations, valid attribution, observed conversion rates, and actual spend—not the planned targets alone.
